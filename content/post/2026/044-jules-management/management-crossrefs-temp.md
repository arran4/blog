### Related implementation and testing references

The management workflow is the coordination layer, not a substitute for repository-specific implementation and testing guidance. When preparing a Jules task or reviewing a pull request, consult the relevant companion posts below and link the applicable guidance in the task or review. Do not attach every article mechanically to every prompt; choose based on the actual code paths and risks, and prefer current repository instructions where they differ.

- **Filesystem behaviour and safe tests:** [Testing File Systems with MockFS and MapFS](/blog/post/2026/033-testing-fs-with-mapfs-mockfs/) explains small filesystem interfaces, production adapters and in-memory test doubles. For code that writes files, prefer deterministic, isolated tests that assert resulting contents and prevent accidental writes to the real environment; retain a focused real-filesystem integration test where actual OS semantics matter. [Treat Side Effects as Dependencies](/blog/post/2026/007-Go-FSs-Everywhere/) gives the wider boundary-setting rationale.
- **Fixtures and scenario evidence:** [Txtar Patterns for Agents](/blog/post/2026/004-txtar-patterns-for-agents/) describes readable input/expected-state archives and scenario fixtures. Use these where they make regressions and generated output reviewable; require assertions of real behaviour rather than accepting a fixture's existence or a passing no-op test as proof.
- **Narrow testability changes:** [Optional Dependency Injection via Type-Switched Variadic Args](/blog/post/2026/020-optional-dependency-injection-via-type-switched-variadic-args/) shows one way to introduce test dependencies without breaking existing callers. Use the smallest suitable seam for the project; do not impose a general abstraction or change public signatures solely to satisfy a test.
- **Agent ownership and recovery:** [ChatGPT-Orchestrated Jules to Agy or Codex](/blog/post/2026/038-jules-agy-codex-handoff/) supplies historical context on independent review and implementation handoffs. The branch-ownership, direct-fix and Ready for review rules in **this** article take precedence if the older account differs.

The common review question is not whether an agent says that it tested a change, but whether the diff and repeatable tests demonstrate the requested input-to-output behaviour without unintended side effects. Ask for the narrowest relevant evidence, and distinguish real filesystem or network integration coverage from in-memory unit coverage instead of treating either as a universal replacement for the other.
